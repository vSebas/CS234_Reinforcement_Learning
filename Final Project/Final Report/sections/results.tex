\section{Results}

\subsection{Offline Training Improved, But Deployment Did Not}

The strongest offline checkpoint in the final repo state is the failure-conditioned fine-tune, which reduced validation loss from the parent run's $0.002835$ to $0.002364$. The lateral-weighted fine-tune also improved over the parent to $0.002773$. By contrast, the post-projection FT2 rerun was worse offline than the parent ($0.003170$).

This ranking does not match deployment behavior. The repo's own analysis identifies \texttt{oval\_fatrop\_improved\_postproj\_ft2\_rerun + projection=soft} as the strongest DT operating point before the failure-conditioned run, even though it was not the best offline model. This is a useful negative result: the project found a meaningful gap between offline imitation metrics and warm-start utility.

\subsection{Completed Projection Ablation}

A completed 10-scenario-per-gate projection ablation compared the parent improved checkpoint and the post-projection FT2 rerun under \texttt{off}, \texttt{soft}, and \texttt{full} projection modes. The repo's final verdict was:
\begin{itemize}
\item best parent regime: \texttt{full},
\item best FT2 regime: \texttt{soft},
\item best DT operating point overall: FT2 + \texttt{soft},
\item no DT variant beat the baseline once warm-start inference time was included,
\item acceptance remained $0\%$ for all default-gate runs,
\item iteration reduction remained $0\%$ for all runs.
\end{itemize}

For example, on the 10-scenario obstacle gate, FT2 + \texttt{soft} had baseline solve time $54.48$s, DT solve time $52.58$s, but DT total time $55.21$s with $0\%$ warm-start acceptance. Similar behavior held on the no-obstacle gate.

\subsection{Corrected Relaxed-Gate Diagnostic}

After fixing the repeated-start confound, the corrected relaxed-gate diagnostic for FT2 + \texttt{soft} showed that acceptance was not literally impossible. On the corrected 10-scenario evaluation:
\begin{itemize}
\item no-obstacle gate: acceptance $20\%$, baseline solve time $49.06$s, DT total time $51.98$s, iterations $206.8$ vs baseline $191.8$;
\item obstacle gate: acceptance $20\%$, baseline solve time $56.36$s, DT total time $60.79$s, iterations $256.0$ vs baseline $230.7$.
\end{itemize}

These runs are important because they separate two claims:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the default gate was partly suppressing acceptance;
\item even when acceptance is relaxed, the accepted DT rollouts were still not solver-helpful on average.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Projection Burden in the Final Smoke Benchmark}

The final failure-conditioned smoke rerun also shows why acceptance remained at $0\%$. On the no-obstacle gate, the mean projection total magnitude was $127.29$, the mean max-step projection was $4.91$, and the mean projection fraction was $0.628$. On the obstacle gate, the corresponding values were $121.03$, $4.90$, and $0.544$. These exceed the default gate thresholds even though fallback was already greatly reduced.

This is an important qualitative shift. Earlier failure patterns were dominated by fallback-heavy instability; the final post-fix runs often fail without much fallback at all.
