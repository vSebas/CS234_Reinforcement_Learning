\begin{abstract}
This project studies whether a Decision Transformer (DT) can provide useful warm-start trajectories for nonlinear minimum-time vehicle trajectory optimization with obstacle avoidance. The intended use case is not open-loop imitation alone, but reducing downstream nonlinear solver work by initializing the optimizer with a rollout conditioned on track geometry, obstacle context, and return-to-go. The final implemented system includes: (i) a direct-collocation trajectory optimizer, (ii) an autoregressive DT warm-start generator with dynamics-in-the-loop rollout, (iii) multiple repair-data pipelines including post-projection and failure-conditioned segments, and (iv) benchmark scripts with acceptance gating, projection diagnostics, and rollout traces.

Across successive training runs, offline validation improved substantially. The best parent model reached validation loss $0.002835$, a lateral-weighted fine-tune reached $0.002773$, and a failure-conditioned fine-tune reached $0.002364$. However, these offline gains did not translate into a clear downstream win. Under the corrected evaluation path, warm-start acceptance remained near $0\%$ under the default gate, iteration reduction remained $0\%$, and no DT variant clearly beat the baseline once warm-start inference time was included. A corrected relaxed-gate diagnostic did admit some runs (up to $20\%$ acceptance on a 10-scenario gate), but those accepted cases were still not solver-helpful on average.

The final diagnosis is that the main remaining deployment bottleneck is lateral rollout behavior rather than optimizer integration alone. Early versions were dominated by fallback-heavy instability in $u_y$ and $r$; after several correctness fixes, the dominant failure shifted to early wrong-side or overly aggressive lateral branch selection that triggered repeated lateral clipping and rejection.
\end{abstract}
